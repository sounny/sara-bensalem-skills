Architectural Premise & Tectonic Challenge

The Atlas Terrace Cultural Pavilion confronts the harsh climatic volatility of the High Atlas foothills, where summer midday peaks exceed 42 degC before plunging to 24 degC at nightfall. Rejecting superficial glazed facades that trigger catastrophic thermal runaway, the project establishes a monumental thermodynamic plinth grounded directly into bedrock shale strata.

Passive evaporative cooling loops inspired by regional Al Falaj channels draw nighttime katabatic airflow across subterranean water reservoirs, circulating cool air through pressurized structural hollow-core slabs. By marrying ancient desert thermodynamic traditions with certified Passivhaus envelope detailing, the building eliminates active mechanical chillers while providing stable 21 degC–25 degC microclimates for delicate historic manuscripts.

Act 2: Territorial & Climatic Context

Diurnal Thermal Damping Strategy

Microclimate data for the Marrakech-Atlas foothills records an extreme 18 Kelvin diurnal temperature swing during the cooling season. To stabilize indoor mean radiant temperatures, the building envelope integrates thick rammed shale exterior walls (350mm) and high-density reinforced concrete shear cores (250mm).

The resulting 11.4-hour thermal wave lag effectively shifts exterior peak heat flux (occurring at 14:00) to the interior surface at 01:30, precisely when cooler night drafts purge the building structure.

Prevailing Wind Vectors & Katabatic Drafts

Nighttime katabatic winds descending from Mount Toubkal are harvested via north-facing Barjeel intake wind towers. These cool drafts are directed downward into subterranean earth tubes, descending 4.5m below grade to strip heat from the thermal mass before exhausting through solar-assisted central thermal chimneys.

Solar Azimuth & Shading Geometry

Solar geometry dictates envelope fenestration. At summer solstice noon, the solar azimuth peaks at 82 deg. Deep 1200mm cantilevered post-tensioned concrete roof slabs and motorized terracotta louver arrays provide 100% solar exclusion across all glazed exposures from May through September.

During winter months, low-angle solar rays (35 deg altitude) penetrate deep into the southern exhibition galleries, charging the dark-pigmented screed floor slabs to provide radiant passive winter heating.

Act 2: Site Morphology & Bioclimatic Systems

Geomorphic Bedrock Coupling

The pavilion terraces naturally along the 14% mountain contour gradient. By partially submerging the lower archival vaults 3.8m into stable shale bedrock, the foundation envelope benefits from year-round ground temperatures of 17.5 degC. Earth coupling provides continuous passive cooling without power consumption.

Al Falaj Subterranean Cooling Concourse

An integrated gravity-fed Al Falaj water channel circulates mountain spring water beneath the public exhibition atrium. Evaporative cooling strips 8.2 degC from incoming fresh air streams while introducing humidity during hyper-arid dry desert winds, raising ambient relative humidity from 18% to a comfortable 45%.

The foundation plinth is elevated 300mm above finished grade on an insulated hydraulic lime footing, establishing an impermeable splash clearance against rare 100-year flash flooding events.

Act 3: Spatial Anatomy & Statutory Egress

Structural Grid System (1–17, A–L)

The building layout is anchored to an orthogonal 7.2m x 7.2m column grid. Transverse axes are designated numerically 1 through 17; longitudinal axes are designated alphabetically A through L. This modular discipline aligns primary load-bearing shear cores with mechanical service shafts, enabling column-free 14.4m gallery spans.

Post-tensioned two-way concrete flat plates (250mm thickness) transfer gravity and seismic forces directly to perimeter shear walls and central cores, eliminating downstand beams and maximizing unencumbered ceiling heights.

Load Path Axonometric Breakdown

Cantilevered balcony verandas extend 3.6m past the southern column line. Load paths transfer through thermal-break structural connectors (Schöck Isokorb) into the main slab, with bending moments resolved through continuous top tensile steel reinforcement anchored into core walls.

Act 4: Tectonic Proof & Hygrothermal Rigor

Glaser Hygrothermal Method (ISO 13788)

Architectural rigor requires verifiable hygrothermal physics. The building envelope was analyzed using the ISO 13788 Glaser method under extreme winter heating conditions (-5 degC / 80% RH exterior, 20 degC / 50% RH interior) and extreme summer cooling conditions (42 degC / 30% RH exterior, 24 degC / 50% RH interior).

By placing 140mm of continuous polyisocyanurate (PIR) insulation outboard of the 250mm concrete shear structure, the structural wall remains fully on the warm side of the dew-point isotherm year-round, eliminating all interstitial condensation risks.
